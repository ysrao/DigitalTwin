Centralized PPO versus Cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for SLA-Protected RAN Slice Allocation

Matched synthetic comparison for eMBB, URLLC, and mIoT/eMTC slice allocation — companion to the Multi-Tier 6G RAN Digital Twin v6 paper

Rao Yenamandra · Mubanga Nsofu · Asokan Ram

Cooperative MARL improves reward

Across 10 training seeds and 40 held-out traffic seeds per trained pair (400 paired evaluations), cooperative MARL raises mean composite reward from 0.9162 to 1.0147. The training-seed-clustered 95% interval for the difference is wholly positive, and modeled interval-SLA compliance improves for all three slices. Resource utilization falls by 1.25 percentage points, so this is a reward-weighted re-prioritization of a fixed budget, not a uniform gain.

What was compared

  • Centralized PPO: one 13-input, two-hidden-layer 64×64 PPO actor–critic chooses a joint feasible allocation template.
  • Cooperative MARL: three 7-input, 64×64 PPO actor–critics — one per slice — submit local bids; a deterministic coordinator projects them onto the same PRB budget and slice floors (0.25, 0.20, 0.10). All three receive the same cooperative reward.
  • Matched controls: 260 training episodes × 48 MDP steps (12,480 interactions) for each of 10 training seeds; the same training traces; 40 held-out evaluation traces per trained pair (400 paired cases).
  • Common objective: 0.35 σeMBB + 0.45 σURLLC + 0.20 σmIoT − SLA deficits + 0.05 utilization − 0.025 allocation churn. SLA targets θ = (0.95, 0.999, 0.99); deficit weights k = (0.70, 1.30, 0.80).
  • Fairness: not in the reward. Service protection is anchored in explicit slice SLA targets.
  • Publication gate: a positive training-seed-clustered 95% interval for the reward difference AND no slice SLA-compliance regression greater than 0.5 percentage point. Both were fixed before the sweep and both are met.

Interpretation limits

This is a reproducible synthetic engineering comparison in a bounded single-cell PRB-share environment — not field-network evidence and not a 3GPP / O-RAN conformance result. It omits the companion twin's six-tier propagation, mobility, association, handover, explicit interference, non-terrestrial dynamics, and multi-access steering, so the gain cannot be cited as an end-to-end six-tier MARL result. The centralized and multi-agent parameterizations are structurally different; matching hidden width and interaction count improves comparability but does not equalize representational capacity. Ten training seeds are useful but modest, and two of them contribute a disproportionate share of the mean gain. The defined next experiment is to integrate the MARL controller into the full twin and repeat the paired, seed-clustered evaluation there.
